# Narration — how to generate it well

**The scripts themselves live in [`video-checklist.md`](video-checklist.md)**,
steps 2 and 3, so there is only one copy of them and it is the one you are
working from on the day. This page is the reasoning behind them.

---

## Why generated narration, and why it is the easier path

It reverses the order of work, in your favour.

**Record the picture silent.** No microphone, no room noise, no plosives, no
neighbour, no performance. Take it as many times as you like; a silent take costs
nothing but disk.

**Then fit the voice to the picture.** If a line lands two seconds early,
regenerate it slightly longer or move it. You are no longer performing to a
stopwatch, which was the hardest part of doing this live.

## Two voices, not one

The narrator and the depot must sound like different people. A viewer who notices
the same voice on both sides of a phone call stops believing the call.

## Why the numbers are spelled out

Every text-to-speech engine mangles `$612` and `BK-7741` eventually. The figures
are what carry the technical argument — six hundred and twelve rejected against a
five hundred limit is the moment a viewer understands that constraints are
checked rather than suggested. A garbled number loses that.

## Why there is silence

The script is about two minutes of speech across a two-fifty-six video. That gap
is deliberate. Wall-to-wall voiceover is what makes a demo feel like an advert,
and silence over a moving timeline reads as confidence.

Keep roughly half a second of quiet at the head of each clip so a line does not
collide with the row appearing underneath it.

## Settings worth starting from

| | |
|---|---|
| Stability | around 50 — steady without going robotic |
| Similarity | 70–80 |
| Style exaggeration | low or zero |
| Speaker boost | on |

Pick a **measured** voice. Demo narration fails far more often from
over-enthusiasm than from being too flat; the material is already interesting.

## Use a stock voice, not a cloned one

The hackathon rules require that a submission not violate anyone's rights of
publicity. A cloned voice of a real person is exactly the sort of thing that
becomes a problem after you have won something. Check your plan permits
commercial use and keep whatever attribution it asks for.

## The two lines that have to land

> But they told it who could. That number came out of the phone call.

> Three companies. I gave it one. It found the other two by asking the people it
> was already talking to.

If either ends on a rising tone, regenerate it. A question intonation on your two
best lines undercuts them both.
